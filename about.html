About this collection

What these essays prove, what they only state, and how each claim was checked. The distinction is the point, so it comes first.

What is proved, and what is not

A series like this can mislead in one specific way: by narrating a proof so smoothly that a reader finishes believing they have seen one. The register therefore answers three separate questions about every claim: whether it is proved or assumed, whether it is available yet, and whether the FLT chain actually needs it. Collapsing those questions into one green check would hide exactly the distinction this site exists to preserve.

The finished proof would rest on 21 assumed results.

These are the results the FLT chain consumes and does not prove. Each is named in the completion policy, which the validator pins to this register, so adding one is a deliberate edit rather than a side effect. This is the only number against which the phrase a derivation of Fermat's Last Theorem from named assumptions is defensible.

Both rosters come from one role field per record, so re-classifying an assumption is a one-word edit and the validator immediately re-pins the completion policy to match. Three of the calls above are judgements rather than obvious facts, and are the ones worth disagreeing with: associativity is on the chain because the group law is what defines torsion and reduction, complex uniformization is off it because nothing in the closing argument evaluates a lattice, and the discriminant criterion is on it because essay 08 reads bad reduction straight off $\Delta_{\min}$. Essays 09 and 23 may move any of the three.

When the required debt is empty — and it is not empty today; the count is generated above — the honest description of this collection will be: a complete and checkable derivation of Fermat's Last Theorem from the assumed results on the FLT-chain list, plus a guided tour of the background around them. Two things that sentence deliberately does not say. It does not say from first principles: the chain rests on the named assumptions, and that list is the honest answer to what this proof stands on. And it does not say from all the assumptions on this page: the background results are not load-bearing, and pooling the two lists into one number would overstate the debt as badly as hiding it would understate it. Assumed results do not turn into proofs when the series is finished; they remain visible in their own register.

The ledger

Every essay ends with a three-column ledger: proved here, imported here, and still owed. A green check is reserved for something the essay actually proves. An imported theorem can make an object available to later essays, but it remains labelled as an assumption. An outline or conditional computation stays in the owed column.

This is not decoration. The failure mode of a long expository chain is an essay that leans on something a later essay builds — a defect no careful reading catches, because each essay is internally correct. The current script checks that every written essay carries a ledger and that its first rung does not link forward. It also checks every proof-status marker against data/ledger.json, including its column, mode and role. Once the intervening essays exist, the stronger carry-forward equality must become a release gate: every required debt is either proved or explicitly accepted by the completion policy, and nothing merely outlined or conditional is allowed to disappear. The ledger already caught one dependency gap while the series was being planned.

Reading order

The essays were not written in reading order. The two ends of the rope came first — Fermat's descent and the closing contradiction — so that every construction in between had a stated destination to justify itself against. Read them in numerical order; that order is the dependency order, which is what the ledger enforces.

Prerequisites

The collection has one global floor: undergraduate linear and abstract algebra, plus a first course in complex analysis. It assumes no algebraic number theory, prior Galois theory beyond field extensions, algebraic geometry, elliptic curves or modular forms. The contents page now gives a more precise prerequisite band for each Part. Those bands are a contract: if a later essay needs more, it must introduce or explicitly add the missing prerequisite rather than leaving the reader to diagnose the gap.

Sources, and a limitation stated plainly

This collection was written without a personal library of the standard textbooks. Rather than cite section numbers from memory — which is how expository errors are manufactured — the citations are tiered.

Cited precisely: entries in the LMFDB by their stable labels, and published papers and surveys by theorem or section number. Labels and paper theorem numbers do not drift between editions, which is exactly the failure that makes a recalled textbook section number untrustworthy. The standing reference for the architecture of Wiles's proof is the expository survey of Darmon, Diamond and Taylor.

Versioned open lecture notes: a numbered pointer is used only when the notes are pinned to a named course offering or version and a stable dated URL. The essay names that version, and SOURCES.md records both its URL and the date it was checked. Rolling, unversioned notes receive topic-level pointers instead.

Cited by topic only: textbooks not consulted directly — Silverman's The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, Diamond and Shurman's A First Course in Modular Forms, and the Cornell–Silverman–Stevens volume. These are named with the topic to look up and no section number, and are marked where they appear. A topic pointer degrades gracefully across editions; a fabricated section number simply sends the reader to the wrong page.

Not cited at all: encyclopaedias, blogs, and lecture videos. They are useful for finding out what a thing is called. They are not evidence.

Numbers are computed, not asserted

Because the usual safety net of a verified page reference is unavailable, every numerical claim is computed from scratch by a script kept alongside the essays, and where possible confirmed a second way. The point counts of a curve are found by brute force and compared against an independently computed $q$-expansion and against the LMFDB entry. The dimension formula for $S_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ is evaluated from the index and the counts of elliptic points and cusps, and cross-checked against the classical list of levels for which it vanishes. A claim carried by two independent computations and a database is on firmer ground than one carried by a page number.

Original prose

All exposition here is written from understanding, not adapted from a source. No passage, derivation or figure from any book, paper or transcript is reproduced. The content of a theorem belongs to everyone; its author's sentences do not. Worked examples are constructed for these essays rather than borrowed.
