Part I · The old method, and why it stops — Essay 05

Galois representations

$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ cannot be written down, but a $2\times2$ matrix can. Turning the one into the other loses information on purpose — and the surprise is how little is lost: two such objects agree as soon as a sequence of integers does.

This essay defines, states, and verifies Defines: a continuous $\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(K)$, unramified primes, irreducibility and semisimplicity. States: inertia subgroups and the conductor $N(\rho)$; and that traces of Frobenius determine a semisimple representation. Verifies: the matrix fact underneath that theorem, and that its semisimplicity hypothesis cannot be dropped.

Rung 1 What we already have

This essay has no forward debts, which has not been true for a while. Both its inputs are written.

Essay 03 gives $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ as $\varprojlim\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$, its topology, and the definition of continuity that goes with a non-discrete target: every layer determined at a finite level, not $\rho$ itself. It gives Frobenius elements at unramified primes as conjugacy classes, and the cyclotomic character with $\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)=\ell$.

Essay 04 gives $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ with its topology, free modules of rank $2$, and the criterion that a matrix lies in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ exactly when its determinant is a unit. It also fixes the convention in force here: $\ell$ is an auxiliary prime, $p$ is the Fermat exponent.

What is missing is the vocabulary in which the rest of the collection speaks. Essay 10 built a representation before this essay existed and had to leave three words undefined — continuous, unramified, conductor. This essay supplies them.

Rung 2 The construction

The definition, and what continuity costs

Let $K$ be a topological ring — for this collection $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ or $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. A two-dimensional Galois representation is a continuous group homomorphism

$$ \rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_2(K). $$

Essay 03 already sorted out what continuity means, and the two cases stay distinct here. For $K=\mathbb{F}_\ell$, discrete and finite, $\rho$ has open kernel and factors through $\mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q})$ for a single finite $K'$: the representation is a finite object in disguise. For $K=\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ it is not, and typically has infinite image; what continuity asks is that each reduction $G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$ be determined at a finite level.

Say plainly what has been given up. $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is uncountable and unpresentable; $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ has $(\ell^2-1)(\ell^2-\ell)$ elements. Almost everything is discarded. The bet of the whole subject is that what survives — where the representation is ramified, what its traces are — is enough to answer arithmetic questions. The rest of this essay is about what survives.

Ramification: where the representation notices a prime

Essay 03 built decomposition groups at unramified primes and stopped there. To speak of an arbitrary prime, one more subgroup is needed.

Stated: inertia, and the conductor. For each prime $\ell$ there is a decomposition subgroup $D_\ell\subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and inside it an inertia subgroup $I_\ell$, with $D_\ell/I_\ell$ generated by $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$. Say $\rho$ is unramified at $\ell$ when $\rho(I_\ell)=1$; then $\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)$ is well defined up to conjugacy. There is a conductor $$N(\rho)=\prod_\ell\ell^{\,n_\ell(\rho)},$$ defined by a formula in the higher ramification subgroups, with two properties this collection uses: $n_\ell=0$ exactly when $\rho$ is unramified at $\ell$, and when the ramification is tame, $$n_\ell=\dim V-\dim V^{I_\ell},$$ the codimension of the subspace that inertia fixes. The general formula is not developed here.

Two remarks make that less abstract than it looks. First, $\rho$ is unramified at all but finitely many primes, so $N(\rho)$ is a genuine integer. Second, the conductor is a measure of how much of the representation a prime disturbs: unramified means the prime is invisible to $\rho$; $n_\ell=1$ means inertia fixes a line but not the plane; $n_\ell=2$ means it fixes nothing.

That middle case is the one the endgame turns on. Essay 24 must show that $\bar\rho_{\mathrm{Frey},p}$ has conductor exactly $2$ — meaning unramified at every odd prime, and at $2$ disturbed just enough to fix a line and no more. The definition needed to state that is now on the page.

Irreducible, reducible, semisimple

Call $\rho$ irreducible if no line in $K^2$ is preserved by every $\rho(\sigma)$, and reducible otherwise. In two dimensions the picture is completely concrete. A preserved line means a basis in which every matrix is upper triangular,

$$ \rho(\sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_1(\sigma)&*\\0&\psi_2(\sigma)\end{pmatrix}, $$

and the diagonal entries are then one-dimensional representations — characters $\psi_i:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to K^\times$. So a reducible two-dimensional representation is two characters plus a possibly non-zero corner.

$\rho$ is semisimple if it is a direct sum of irreducibles: in dimension two, either irreducible, or a direct sum $\psi_1\oplus\psi_2$ of two characters — the reducible case with the corner made zero. Every $\rho$ has a semisimplification: keep the diagonal, discard the corner. That operation is not invertible, and the next section is about exactly the information it destroys.

Two invariants survive any change of basis, because they are conjugation-invariant: $\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma)$ and $\det\rho(\sigma)$. And this is where essay 03's remark pays: $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ is only a conjugacy class, so trace and determinant are precisely the quantities one is allowed to evaluate on it. Essay 10 wrote $\operatorname{tr}\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)$ and $\det\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)$ for that reason, and could not have written the matrix.

Traces are enough

Now the theorem that makes the whole collection's strategy viable.

Stated. Let $\rho_1,\rho_2:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(K)$ be continuous and semisimple, both unramified outside a finite set $S$. If $$\operatorname{tr}\rho_1(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)=\operatorname{tr}\rho_2(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell) \quad\text{for all }\ell\notin S,$$ then $\rho_1\cong\rho_2$. Two ingredients: the Frobenius classes are dense in $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, so knowing traces there fixes traces everywhere; and a semisimple representation is determined by its trace function.

Read what that buys. A representation is an uncountable amount of data. The theorem says that for semisimple ones, a sequence of numbers indexed by primes determines it completely. So the assertion "the representation coming from this elliptic curve is the one coming from that modular form" — which is what modularity says in essay 18 — becomes the assertion that two integer sequences agree. That is checkable, and essay 18 checks it.

The hypothesis is not decoration, and it is worth seeing why. Consider

$$ \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. $$

Same trace, same determinant, same characteristic polynomial — and not conjugate, since one is the identity and the other is not. The second is reducible but not semisimple: it fixes a line and cannot be diagonalised. So traces cannot distinguish a representation from its semisimplification, and any theorem claiming they could would be false. What the theorem identifies is semisimplifications, which is all the Fermat argument ever needs.

Rung 3 What it buys

The smallest interesting representations are quadratic fields

A one-dimensional representation is a character $\psi:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to K^\times$, and the concrete ones are already familiar. Essay 03 computed $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/5)^\times$, cyclic of order $4$. Compose with the unique surjection to $\{\pm1\}$ — the Legendre symbol $a\mapsto\left(\tfrac a5\right)$:

$a$$1$$2$$3$$4$
$\left(\tfrac a5\right)$$+1$$-1$$-1$$+1$

Its kernel is $\{1,4\}=\{1,\sigma_{-1}\}$ — precisely the subgroup essay 03 showed fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$. So this character is that quadratic field, viewed as a representation, and it is ramified only at $5$: its conductor is $5$. A character of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with values in $\{\pm1\}$ and a quadratic field are the same information.

Identifying a curve's representation by counting points

Here is the theorem doing work on an object this collection has already computed.

Essay 10 found that the conductor-$11$ curve $y^2+y=x^3-x^2$ has a rational subgroup of order $5$ — essay 07's point $(0,0)$ — so $\bar\rho_{E,5}$ is reducible. Because those five points are rational, $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ fixes them one by one, so the stable line carries the trivial character; and $\det=\chi$ from essay 10 forces the other diagonal entry. Hence the semisimplification is

$$ \bar\rho_{E,5}^{\,\mathrm{ss}}\;\cong\;\mathbf{1}\oplus\bar\chi, $$

whose trace at $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ is $1+\ell$ — using essay 03's $\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)=\ell$. Essay 10 checked $a_\ell\equiv1+\ell\pmod 5$ at all $44$ good primes below $200$. On its own that was a numerical coincidence with a plausible explanation. With the theorem in hand it is an identification: any semisimple $\bar\rho$ with those traces is isomorphic to $\mathbf{1}\oplus\bar\chi$, so that is what the semisimplification is.

Notice that the theorem's hypothesis is doing real work rather than being satisfied by luck. The conclusion is about the semisimplification only — the actual $\bar\rho_{E,5}$ might have a non-zero corner, and no amount of point counting could detect it. The theorem is stated at exactly the strength the evidence supports.

And the check is not vacuous: the same congruence fails at more than twenty primes for $y^2=x^3+1$, which has no rational subgroup of order $5$.

What the rest of the collection collects

  • Essay 10 is now complete in one half. It built $\rho_{E,\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ and called it continuous before continuity was defined; the definition above is the one it needs, in the layer-wise form. Its trace formula remains an import — that needs elliptic curves over finite fields, not representation theory, so it belongs to a retrofit of essay 08 rather than to this essay.
  • Essay 16 attaches a representation to a modular form. Its output is described by traces, which is only useful because of the theorem above.
  • Essay 18 states modularity as an isomorphism of representations. The theorem above is what turns that into the checkable claim that $a_\ell(E)=a_\ell(f)$ for all $\ell$, and essay 18's worked example is that check at level $11$.
  • Essay 24 needs the conductor, defined above, and needs irreducibility, which Mazur's theorem in essay 10 supplies. Its whole task is to evaluate $N(\bar\rho_{\mathrm{Frey},p})$ and get $2$.

Rung 4 Reading

Galois representations, irreducibility, and oddness. Andrew Sutherland's MIT 18.783 Fall 2023 Lecture 25, §25.3, constructs $\rho_{E,\ell}$ and $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$ and fixes the notion of isomorphism used here, $\rho'(\sigma)=A\rho(\sigma)A^{-1}$; its §25.4 defines an irreducible representation as one whose image fixes no one-dimensional subspace, equivalently is not conjugate into the upper-triangular matrices, and defines what it means for a mod $\ell$ representation to be modular by comparing $\operatorname{tr}\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)$ with $a_p$ — the comparison this essay's trace theorem licenses.

Serre's conductor recipe. The same lecture's §25.4 gives, for an irreducible mod $\ell$ representation attached to a semistable curve, the optimal level as the product of the primes with valuation of $\Delta_{\min}$ not divisible by the residual prime. In this collection's convention that reads $\prod\ell$ over the $\ell$ with $v_\ell(\Delta_{\min})\not\equiv0\pmod p$ — the source uses the opposite letters, $\ell$ for the residual prime and $p$ for the auxiliary one, so the roles must be swapped when quoting it.

Absolute values, inertia and the local picture. Andrew Sutherland's MIT 18.785 Fall 2021 Lecture 8, §8.2, treats valuation rings, uniformizers and completions, which is the local setting in which inertia subgroups and the higher ramification filtration are defined.

Further reference. A course in algebraic number theory constructs inertia subgroups and the ramification filtration; the conductor of a representation and the theorem that traces determine a semisimple representation are standard in the representation-theoretic literature.

Check it yourself. Run checks/representations.py. It enumerates $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ for $\ell\leq7$ and confirms that the diagonalisable matrices with a given characteristic polynomial form a single conjugacy class — the matrix fact under the trace theorem — and that the identity and the Jordan block share a characteristic polynomial without being conjugate, which is why the semisimplicity hypothesis cannot be dropped. It then checks that the quadratic character of $(\mathbb{Z}/5)^\times$ is a homomorphism with kernel $\{1,4\}$, and that $\mathbf{1}\oplus\bar\chi$ predicts the conductor-$11$ curve's $a_\ell$ modulo $5$ at every good prime while failing for a curve without a rational $5$-subgroup.
