Part I · The old method, and why it stops — Essay 03

The absolute Galois group

Essay 02 adjoined a cube root of unity to $\mathbb{Q}$ and used it without ever asking what its symmetries were. Ask, and one group appears that contains every such symmetry at once — infinite, unpresentable, and assembled entirely from finite pieces.

This essay constructs, computes, and states Constructs: $G_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ as an inverse limit of finite Galois groups, its topology, and what continuity means against a non-discrete target. Computes: $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$ with $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell=\sigma_\ell$, so the cyclotomic character satisfies $\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)=\ell$. States: the existence of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, the Galois correspondence, irreducibility of $\Phi_n$, and the construction of Frobenius elements at unramified primes.

Rung 1 What we already have

Essay 02 did all of this essay's motivating work without naming it. It adjoined $\omega$, a primitive cube root of unity, to $\mathbb{Q}$ and worked in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$. It used the fact that $\omega$ satisfies $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$ and that $\omega$ and $\omega^2$ are interchangeable — the norm $N(a+b\omega)=a^2-ab+b^2$ is exactly the product of $a+b\omega$ with $a+b\omega^2$. That interchange is a symmetry of the field, and this essay is about the group of all such symmetries.

Essay 02 also found that $3$ behaves specially in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$: it is not a product of two distinct primes but $-\omega^2\lambda^2$ with $\lambda=1-\omega$. That is ramification, and it will be exactly the case this essay's construction has to exclude.

Essay 01 is not used. Nor is essay 04: the two are deliberately independent, and a reader who took the number systems first has lost nothing.

Assumed of the reader: groups, rings, fields, ideals and quotients, and the definition of a field extension. No Galois theory beyond that definition.

Rung 2 The construction

Extensions, degree, and their automorphisms

A field $K$ containing $\mathbb{Q}$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$, and its degree $[K:\mathbb{Q}]$ is that dimension. For $K=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$, the relation $\omega^2=-1-\omega$ means $1,\omega$ already spans, so the degree is $2$.

Write $\mathrm{Aut}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ for the field automorphisms of $K$ fixing every rational number. Such a $\sigma$ is heavily constrained: it must send a root of a rational polynomial to another root of the same polynomial, because applying $\sigma$ to $f(\alpha)=0$ gives $f(\sigma\alpha)=0$. So an automorphism can only permute the roots of each rational polynomial — that single observation is the whole subject in miniature.

For $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$ the possibilities are $\omega\mapsto\omega$ and $\omega\mapsto\omega^2$, the two roots of $x^2+x+1$. Both occur, so $\#\mathrm{Aut}=2=[K:\mathbb{Q}]$, and the extension is called Galois. The group is written $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$.

It does not always work out. Take $K=\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$, of degree $3$. The other two roots of $x^3-2$ are complex, and $K$ consists of real numbers, so there is nowhere for $2^{1/3}$ to go: $\#\mathrm{Aut}(K/\mathbb{Q})=1\lt3$. The extension is not Galois. Being Galois means the field is large enough to contain all the roots it needs.

Stated: the Galois correspondence. For a finite Galois extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$, the subgroups $H\leq\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ correspond one-to-one with the intermediate fields $\mathbb{Q}\subseteq F\subseteq K$, by sending $H$ to its fixed field and $F$ to the automorphisms fixing $F$. The correspondence reverses inclusions and satisfies $[K:F]=\#H$. Not proved here; it is the standard theorem of a first course.

All the algebraic numbers at once

Each finite extension sees only some polynomials. To see all of them, take $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, the algebraic closure: the field of all complex numbers satisfying some non-zero rational polynomial. Every rational polynomial factors into linear factors over it.

Stated. $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ exists, is algebraically closed, and is the union of all finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$ inside $\mathbb{C}$.

The absolute Galois group is

$$ G_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) =\{\text{field automorphisms of }\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\text{ fixing }\mathbb{Q}\}. $$

This object is the reason the rest of the collection exists, and it is worth being blunt about why it cannot be studied directly. It is infinite, in fact uncountable. It has no finite presentation. Its elements cannot be written down: specifying one means choosing, coherently, a permutation of the roots of every rational polynomial at once. Nobody computes in $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ by manipulating elements.

Assembled from finite pieces

What saves the situation is that although $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is not finite, it is built out of groups that are. Let $\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and let $K/\mathbb{Q}$ be finite Galois. Since $\sigma$ permutes the roots of each rational polynomial, it maps $K$ to itself, so it restricts to an element $\sigma|_K\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$. These restrictions are compatible: if $K\subseteq L$ then $(\sigma|_L)|_K=\sigma|_K$.

Conversely a compatible family of automorphisms, one for each finite Galois $K$, determines $\sigma$ — because every algebraic number lies in some finite $K$, so its image is already decided. Hence

$$ \boxed{\quad G_{\mathbb{Q}}\;\cong\;\varprojlim_K\ \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}),\quad} $$

the inverse limit over finite Galois extensions ordered by inclusion. A group of this shape is called profinite.

Compare essay 04, where $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ was built as $\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n\mathbb{Z}$: an element was a coherent choice of residue at every precision. Here an element is a coherent choice of root-permutation in every finite extension. The two constructions are the same manoeuvre, and neither essay needs the other — which is why they can be read in either order.

The inverse limit also supplies a topology, and this is what makes the word continuous mean something in essay 05. Declare the subgroups $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)$, for $K$ finite Galois, to be open, and let the topology be generated by their cosets.

What continuity then means depends on the target, and it is worth being careful because the tempting answer is wrong for the object this collection actually uses. When the target is discrete — as it is for $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$, whose target $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is a finite group — continuity does say what one hopes: the kernel is open, so the map is determined at a finite level and factors through $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ for a single finite $K$.

But $\rho_{E,\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ is not of that kind, and it is the main object of essay 10. It is continuous and yet factors through no finite quotient at all: for a curve without complex multiplication its image is open in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$, hence infinite. The reason is that the target is not discrete — $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ carries the $\ell$-adic topology of essay 04.

The correct formulation uses both towers, and this is where the parallel with essay 04 stops being decorative. Each side is an inverse limit,

$$ G_{\mathbb{Q}}=\varprojlim_K\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}), \qquad \mathbb{Z}_\ell=\varprojlim_n\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n\mathbb{Z}, $$

and $\rho$ is continuous exactly when it respects both: every reduction $G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$ is determined at a finite level, while $\rho$ itself need not be. Finite level on each layer, not finite overall. That is the definition essay 05 inherits, and the reason $\bar\rho$ is the easier object: it is a single layer, so for it the two formulations coincide.

Frobenius: one element per prime, up to conjugacy

Individual elements of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ are inaccessible, with one crucial family of exceptions. Each prime supplies one, and those are the elements every later essay evaluates.

Fix a finite Galois $K/\mathbb{Q}$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, and a prime $\ell$ that is unramified in $K$ — meaning $\ell\mathcal{O}_K$ factors into distinct prime ideals, $\ell\mathcal{O}_K=\mathfrak{P}_1\cdots\mathfrak{P}_r$. This is the condition essay 02's prime $3$ fails in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, where the two factors of $\lambda$ coincide.

Stated: the Frobenius package. $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ permutes the $\mathfrak{P}_i$ transitively. The stabiliser of one of them, the decomposition group $D_{\mathfrak{P}}$, maps isomorphically onto $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}/\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ when $\ell$ is unramified — and that group is cyclic, generated by $x\mapsto x^\ell$. The preimage of that generator is the Frobenius element $\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}}$.

Transitivity has an immediate consequence that matters more than it looks. Different choices of $\mathfrak{P}$ give conjugate Frobenius elements, so

$$ \mathrm{Frob}_\ell\ \text{is a conjugacy class, not an element.} $$

Keep that in view. Every use of Frobenius in this collection is a use of something conjugation-invariant — which is exactly why essay 10 could speak of $\operatorname{tr}\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)$ and $\det\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)$, and could not have spoken of the matrix itself.

Rung 3 What it buys

The one family where everything is explicit

Let $\zeta=\zeta_n$ be a primitive $n$-th root of unity and $K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$. An automorphism must send $\zeta$ to another primitive $n$-th root, so $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^a$ for some $a$ coprime to $n$; and composing two of them multiplies the exponents. So $\sigma_a\sigma_b=\sigma_{ab}$ and

$$ \mathrm{Gal}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)\;\cong\;(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times, $$

of order $\varphi(n)$ — given that every such $a$ really occurs, which is the statement that the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_n$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. That we state.

Now the payoff. In this family the Frobenius element is not merely constructible, it is obvious: reduction modulo $\ell$ sends $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^\ell$, so

$$ \boxed{\quad\mathrm{Frob}_\ell=\sigma_\ell,\quad\text{that is}\quad \mathrm{Frob}_\ell(\zeta)=\zeta^{\ell}.\quad} $$

The map $\sigma_a\mapsto a$ is the cyclotomic character $\chi$, and therefore $\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)=\ell$. Essay 10 asserted exactly that, when it wrote $\det\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)\equiv\ell$ after identifying $\det\rho$ with $\chi$. Here is where the assertion comes from, and it is a one-line computation.

Because the group is abelian, conjugacy classes are single elements, so in this family $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ genuinely is one automorphism.

Frobenius predicts how primes factor

The order of $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ in the Galois group is the degree of the residue field extension, so it says how $\ell$ splits. For $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$, where the group is cyclic of order $4$:

$\ell$$\ell\bmod5$order of $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$how $\ell$ factors
$11$$1$$1$four primes — splits completely
$19$$4$$2$two primes of degree $2$
$2$$2$$4$one prime — inert
$3$$3$$4$inert

This is checkable without any algebraic number theory, by factoring $\Phi_5=x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1$ modulo $\ell$. Modulo $11$ the fifth roots of unity are $1,3,9,5,4$, so $\Phi_5\equiv(x-3)(x-4)(x-5)(x-9)$ — four linear factors, matching $\mathrm{Frob}_{11}=\sigma_1=\mathrm{id}$. Modulo $2$ it is irreducible, matching an element of order $4$. The general statement, verified in the script for many $n$ and $\ell$, is that the irreducible factors of $\Phi_n$ modulo $\ell$ all have degree equal to the multiplicative order of $\ell$ modulo $n$.

Read that backwards and it becomes the reason this collection cares. A statement about Frobenius is a statement about congruences. "$\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ is trivial in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$" says $\ell\equiv1\pmod n$. Essay 02 met a case of this without the vocabulary: in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, which is $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$, a prime splits exactly when $\ell\equiv1\pmod 3$, and $3$ itself is the ramified exception.

The correspondence, on one field

$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathbb{Q})$ is cyclic of order $4$, so it has exactly one proper non-trivial subgroup, $\{1,\sigma_{-1}\}$, and the correspondence therefore predicts exactly one intermediate field, of degree $2$. Find it: $\sigma_{-1}$ sends $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}$, so it fixes

$$ \alpha=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}. $$

Squaring, $\alpha^2=\zeta^2+2+\zeta^{-2}$, and since $\zeta+\zeta^2+\zeta^3+\zeta^4=-1$ we get $\alpha^2+\alpha-1=0$. So $\alpha=\tfrac{-1\pm\sqrt5}{2}$ and the intermediate field is

$$ \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5). $$

One subgroup, one subfield, and the subfield is one a reader already knows.

What the next essays collect

  • Essay 05 takes the topology and defines a continuous $\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(K)$, together with what it means for such a representation to be ramified at a prime and what its conductor is. It also takes the fact that $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ is a conjugacy class, which is why traces are the right invariant to record.
  • Essay 10 becomes fully grounded. It built $\rho_{E,\ell}$ using only $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ as a group of automorphisms, which needed nothing from here, but it had to state $\operatorname{tr}\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)\equiv a_\ell$ because $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ did not yet exist. Now it does, and the determinant half of that statement — $\det\equiv\ell$ — is the computation $\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)=\ell$ above.
  • Essays 16 and 18 compare two representations by comparing traces of Frobenius. That is only a sensible thing to do because of the conjugacy-class remark; essay 05 turns it into a theorem.

Rung 4 Reading

Frobenius elements, decomposition groups, and the profinite structure. Andrew Sutherland's MIT 18.783 Fall 2023 Lecture 25, §25.3, sets out exactly the package used here: for a prime unramified in $K$, the factorisation $p\mathcal{O}_K=\mathfrak{p}_1\cdots\mathfrak{p}_r$ into distinct primes — for which it refers back to its own Lecture 20 rather than proving it there — the transitive action of $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ on those primes, the decomposition group $D_{\mathfrak{p}}$ as a stabiliser, its isomorphism onto $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_p)$, that group's generation by the $p$-power map, and the resulting definition of $\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ — noting that different choices of $\mathfrak{p}$ give conjugates, so that $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is a conjugacy class. It also treats $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ as a profinite group whose topology is what continuity of $\rho$ refers to.

Absolute values and the ramified case. Andrew Sutherland's MIT 18.785 Fall 2021 Lecture 1, definition 1.7, gives the $p$-adic valuation whose vanishing or not is what ramification measures; that course's Lecture 8, §8.2, treats valuation rings and uniformizers, the local picture behind the decomposition group.

Further reference. Any first course in Galois theory proves the correspondence and the irreducibility of the cyclotomic polynomials; a first course in algebraic number theory proves the Frobenius package.

Check it yourself. Run checks/galois.py. It builds $\Phi_n$ from scratch by dividing $x^n-1$ by the lower cyclotomic polynomials, confirms $\deg\Phi_n=\varphi(n)$, verifies that $\sigma_a\sigma_b=\sigma_{ab}$ gives $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$ as a full multiplication table, and then tests the Frobenius claim the only way it can be tested — by computing, for many $n$ and $\ell$, the least $d$ with $x^{\ell^d}=x$ in $\mathbb{F}_\ell[x]/\Phi_n$ and checking it equals the multiplicative order of $\ell$ modulo $n$. It also confirms the splitting extremes and that $\alpha=\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}$ satisfies $x^2+x-1$.
