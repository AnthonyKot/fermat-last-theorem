Part II · Elliptic curves — Essay 10

The representation attached to a curve

The torsion points have algebraic coordinates, so the symmetries of the algebraic numbers move them around. Write that motion in a basis and the curve has become a family of $2\times2$ matrices — the object the rest of this collection is about.

This essay constructs, derives, and states Constructs: $\rho_{E,\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ and its reduction $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$. Derives: $\det\rho_{E,\ell}$ is the cyclotomic character, hence $\bar\rho$ is odd; and that a rational subgroup of order $\ell$ is exactly what makes $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$ reducible. States: the Weil pairing, the trace formula $\operatorname{tr}\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)\equiv a_\ell$, Mazur's isogeny theorem, and Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich.

Rung 1 What we already have

Essay 09 is the direct input: $E[\ell^n]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)^2$, and the Tate module $T_\ell(E)=\varprojlim E[\ell^n]$ is free of rank $2$ over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. It also computed the rational torsion of two curves — none at $2$ for the conductor-$11$ curve, all of it for the Frey curve — and those two facts become two representations of opposite character below.

Essay 04 gives $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, free modules, and the criterion that a matrix lies in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ exactly when its determinant is a unit. Essay 08 gives $a_\ell=\ell+1-\#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ and the good primes. Essay 07 gives the group law, which is what makes the action below a homomorphism rather than a mere permutation.

One debt, and it is not where it first appeared. This essay was written before essays 03 and 05, and originally declared both as debts. Both now exist and both precede it, so $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, Frobenius elements, continuity and the conductor of a representation are all available — see essays 03 and 05. What remains stated rather than derived is the trace formula below, and the reason is not representation theory at all: it needs elliptic curves over finite fields, specifically that reduction is injective on $p$-torsion at a good prime and that the Frobenius endomorphism satisfies $x^2-a_\ell x+\ell$. That belongs to a retrofit of essay 08, and is scheduled as one.

Rung 2 The construction

The action needs no new ideas

Let $\sigma$ be an automorphism of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ fixing $\mathbb{Q}$, and let it act on points coordinatewise: $\sigma(x,y)=(\sigma x,\sigma y)$, with $\sigma O=O$. Three observations, each immediate:

  • $\sigma$ maps $E$ to itself. The equation has rational coefficients, so applying $\sigma$ to $y^2+a_1xy+a_3y=x^3+\cdots$ leaves it unchanged.
  • $\sigma$ respects addition. Essay 07's formulas for $P+Q$ are rational functions of the coordinates with rational coefficients, and $\sigma$ is a field homomorphism, so $\sigma(P+Q)=\sigma P+\sigma Q$.
  • $\sigma$ preserves $E[n]$. From additivity, $\sigma(nP)=n\,\sigma(P)$, so $nP=O$ implies $n\,\sigma P=O$.

So $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ acts on each $E[\ell^n]$ by group automorphisms. Essay 09 gave $E[\ell^n]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)^2$; choosing a basis compatibly in $n$ and using $T_\ell(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2$, the action becomes a homomorphism

$$ \boxed{\quad\rho_{E,\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow \mathrm{Aut}\bigl(T_\ell(E)\bigr)\cong\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell).\quad} $$

Reducing the entries modulo $\ell$ — equivalently, acting on the single layer $E[\ell]$ — gives the mod $\ell$ representation

$$ \bar\rho_{E,\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_\ell). $$

Nothing in that construction was hard. The object at the centre of the whole Fermat argument is the observation that Galois symmetry moves torsion points, written in a basis. Everything difficult lies in what the resulting matrices are.

The determinant is forced

A $2\times2$ matrix has two obvious invariants, and one of them can be identified outright. The tool is a pairing on torsion.

Stated: the Weil pairing. For each $n$ there is a map $e_n:E[n]\times E[n]\to\mu_n$ into the $n$-th roots of unity which is bilinear, alternating, non-degenerate, and Galois-equivariant: $e_n(\sigma P,\sigma Q)=\sigma\bigl(e_n(P,Q)\bigr)$. Its construction uses divisors on the curve and is not given here.

Take a basis $P,Q$ of $E[n]$. Non-degeneracy makes $\zeta=e_n(P,Q)$ a primitive $n$-th root of unity. Now compute $\sigma\zeta$ two ways. By equivariance it is $e_n(\sigma P,\sigma Q)$; and writing $\sigma$ as the matrix $\rho(\sigma)$ in that basis, bilinearity and alternation give the standard identity

$$ e_n(Mv,Mw)=e_n(v,w)^{\det M}. $$

Both routes must agree, so

$$ \sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{\det\rho(\sigma)}. $$

The exponent by which $\sigma$ acts on roots of unity is called the cyclotomic character $\chi$. So

$$ \boxed{\quad\det\rho_{E,\ell}=\chi_\ell,\quad} $$

the same for every elliptic curve. Half the matrix is fixed before the curve is even named. What distinguishes one curve from another is the trace.

Oddness, in two lines

Complex conjugation restricts to an automorphism $c\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ of order two, and its effect on roots of unity is inversion, $c(\zeta)=\bar\zeta=\zeta^{-1}$. So $\chi(c)=-1$, and therefore

$$ \det\bar\rho_{E,\ell}(c)=-1. $$

A representation with $\det\rho(c)=-1$ is called odd. This is not a decoration: it is one of the hypotheses of Ribet's theorem in essay 25, and it is now discharged for every elliptic curve at once. Note where it came from — the pairing lands in roots of unity, and conjugation inverts them. Oddness is a fact about $\mathbb{C}$, not about the curve.

The trace is the point count

At a prime $\ell$ of good reduction, essay 03's Frobenius element $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ acts, and the matrix it produces is determined by two numbers.

Stated, pending the essay-08 retrofit. For $E$ with good reduction at $\ell$ and $\ell\neq p$, the representation $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ is unramified at $\ell$ and $$ \operatorname{tr}\bar\rho_{E,p}(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)\equiv a_\ell, \qquad \det\bar\rho_{E,p}(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)\equiv\ell \pmod p, $$ so $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ has characteristic polynomial $x^2-a_\ell x+\ell$ modulo $p$.

The determinant half of that is the cyclotomic character evaluated at $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$, which is $\ell$ — consistent with what was just derived. The trace half is the bridge the whole collection is built to cross: the integers $a_\ell$ that essay 08 obtained by counting points are the traces of a family of matrices. Part IV finds a second, entirely different source of the same integers.

Reducible means a rational subgroup

Call $\bar\rho$ reducible if some one-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{F}_\ell^2$ is preserved by every $\sigma$ — equivalently, if the image can be written with all matrices upper triangular. Unwinding the definitions, a Galois-stable line in $E[\ell]$ is a subgroup of order $\ell$ carried to itself by every automorphism, which is what it means for that subgroup to be defined over $\mathbb{Q}$:

$$ \bar\rho_{E,\ell}\text{ reducible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\text{ has a rational subgroup of order }\ell. $$

This is the sentence that makes essay 09's two computations matter. A rational subgroup is a concrete, checkable thing; irreducibility of a representation sounds abstract; they are the same condition. And it is why the two named theorems below are about subgroups when what the argument needs is a statement about representations.

Stated: Mazur's isogeny theorem (1978). No elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ admits a rational subgroup of prime order $\ell$ for $$\ell\notin\{2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,37,43,67,163\},$$ and four of them — $19$, $43$, $67$, $163$ — occur only for curves with complex multiplication. (They are not the last four: $37$ is not a CM case. Its two exceptional $j$-invariants, like those at $11$ and $17$, belong to curves without complex multiplication.) For a semistable curve the list collapses to $\ell\leq7$. Hence $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$ is irreducible for $\ell\gt7$ when $E$ is semistable.

Stated: the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion. For $\ell\neq p$, the representation $\rho_{E,p}$ is unramified at $\ell$ if and only if $E$ has good reduction at $\ell$. So the primes where the representation is ramified are exactly the bad primes — with $p$ itself always requiring separate treatment.

Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich is the reason essay 08's work was not merely local colour: it converts the reduction types computed there into ramification of the representation, which is what essay 24 needs to compute a conductor.

Rung 3 What it buys

Essay 08's mystery, solved — and it is a falsifiable prediction

Essay 08 recorded, without explanation, that every point count of the conductor-$11$ curve was divisible by $5$. Here is the reason, and it is a good advertisement for the whole apparatus.

Essay 07 found a rational point of order $5$, so $\langle(0,0)\rangle$ is a rational subgroup of order $5$ and $\bar\rho_{E,5}$ is reducible. More than that: the subgroup consists of points with rational coordinates, so every $\sigma$ fixes each of them pointwise. The stable line therefore carries the trivial character. In a basis whose first vector spans that line,

$$ \bar\rho_{E,5}(\sigma)= \begin{pmatrix}1&*\\0&\chi(\sigma)\end{pmatrix}, $$

the lower-right entry being forced by $\det=\chi$. Taking traces at $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$:

$$ a_\ell\equiv1+\ell\pmod 5\qquad\text{for every good prime }\ell. $$

Which rearranges to $5\mid\ell+1-a_\ell=\#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ — exactly essay 08's observation. Check it against that essay's table:

$\ell$$a_\ell$$a_\ell\bmod5$$\ell+1\bmod5$
$2$$-2$$3$$3$
$3$$-1$$4$$4$
$7$$-2$$3$$3$
$13$$4$$4$$4$

It holds at all $44$ good primes below $200$. And the prediction has teeth, which is the part that makes it evidence rather than bookkeeping: the same congruence modulo $3$ fails for this curve at $26$ of those primes, because it has no rational $3$-torsion. Run the experiment the other way and it flips — $y^2=x^3+1$, which essay 09 showed has rational $3$-torsion, satisfies the congruence mod $3$ everywhere and fails it mod $5$ at $38$ primes.

Two curves, two primes each, and the congruence holds in exactly the two cases where a rational subgroup exists. That is the cheapest available demonstration that these representations are real objects with observable consequences, rather than a change of language.

The Frey curve's mod $2$ representation is trivial

Essay 09 proved the Frey curve has full rational $2$-torsion. So every point of $E[2]$ is fixed by every automorphism, and

$$ \bar\rho_{E,2}=\text{the trivial representation}. $$

Not merely reducible — trivial, the whole image being the identity. The Frey curve is therefore as far from irreducible as a curve can be at the prime $2$, which is exactly why the Fermat argument works at the prime $p$ instead, and why Mazur's theorem is needed there.

What essay 24 collects

Everything essay 24 needs about the Frey representation is now stated or derived. It is odd, because every $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$ is. Its determinant is $\chi$. It is irreducible for $p\gt7$ by Mazur, given essay 23's semistability — and whether the Frey curve's full rational $2$-torsion pushes that to $p\geq5$ remains open. It is unramified outside $2$, $p$ and the bad primes by Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich, applied to essay 23's reduction types. What remains for essay 24 is the conductor: to show that the odd primes drop out and only $2$ survives.

Rung 4 Reading

The representation, the Tate module, and the trace formula. Andrew Sutherland's MIT 18.783 Fall 2023 Lecture 25, §25.3, constructs the action of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})$ on $E[\ell]$, defines $\rho_{E,\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ from the Tate module, records the characteristic polynomial of $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ as $x^2-(\operatorname{tr})x+\det$ with $\operatorname{tr}\equiv a_p$ and $\det\equiv p$, and defines $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$ by reduction. Its §25.4 defines irreducibility, notes that $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$ is irreducible exactly when $E$ admits no rational $\ell$-isogeny, and gives Mazur's exceptional list $\{2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,37,43,67,163\}$, naming $19$, $43$, $67$ and $163$ as the cases requiring complex multiplication; the same section derives oddness from the complex-conjugation action on the torus.

Semistability sharpens Mazur. Darmon, Diamond and Taylor's survey Fermat's Last Theorem, p. 8–9, states that a semistable elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ has no rational cyclic subgroup of order $\ell$ for $\ell\gt7$, and that this is what gives irreducibility of $\bar\rho_{E,\ell}$ there.

The Weil pairing and Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich. Silverman's The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves constructs the Weil pairing and proves the criterion relating good reduction to unramifiedness.

Check it yourself. Run checks/mod_ell_representation.py. It verifies the pairing identity $e(Mv,Mw)=e(v,w)^{\det M}$ exhaustively over every invertible matrix for several moduli — the algebra that forces $\det\rho=\chi$ — confirms that $-1$ is the unique exponent inverting all roots of unity, and tests the reducibility congruence $a_\ell\equiv\ell+1$ on both curves at both primes, so that it passes in the two cases with a rational subgroup and fails in the two without.
