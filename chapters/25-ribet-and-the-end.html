Part VI · The contradiction — Essay 25

Ribet, and the end

Everything built so far converges on a single object: a weight-2 newform of level two. It must exist. The space it would live in has dimension zero.

This essay states, computes, and concludes conditionally States: Ribet's level-lowering theorem, with hypotheses. Computes: $\dim S_2(\Gamma_0(2)) = 0$, from the dimension formula essay 13 derives. Concludes conditionally: Fermat's Last Theorem.

Rung 1 What we already have

From essay 22: assuming a primitive solution $a^p + b^p = c^p$ with $p \geq 5$ prime, normalised so that $b$ is even and $a^p \equiv -1 \pmod 4$, the Frey curve

$$ E \;:\; y^2 = x(x-a^p)(x+b^p) $$

is an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ with discriminant $\Delta = 16\,(abc)^{2p}$.

One assumption, and it is about the modular side. Everything on the elliptic-curve side of the argument is established, and the one remaining assumption lives in an essay that is not yet written:
  • (M) Modularity. Every semistable elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is modular. Stated in essay 18, applied in essay 23, proved nowhere here.
(F), the dimension formula, used to sit beside it and no longer does. Essay 13 derives $\dim S_2(\Gamma_0(N))=1+\frac{\mu}{12}-\frac{\nu_2}{4}-\frac{\nu_3}{3}-\frac{\nu_\infty}{2}$ from Riemann–Hurwitz, so the number this essay evaluates is now a consequence of essay 11's four counts rather than something quoted. Two imports from Riemann surface theory replace it, and they are counted in the wider number below.

Ribet's theorem is stated in this essay and not proved, and essay 24 assumes one more thing — that $\bar\rho$ is finite at $p$. So this essay's own argument rests on three named inputs: (M), Ribet, and finiteness at $p$. That is the narrower of two numbers on this page, and it answers "what does the last step still take on trust".

It would be wrong to add "and nothing else". The chain reaching this page also spends every assumption the earlier essays made — the Weil pairing, the trace formula, Mazur, inertia and the conductor, and the rest. The wider number answers "what does the finished proof stand on", and it is the one to quote if anyone asks. The finished proof would rest on 21 assumed results in total — see About for the roster. That number is generated from data/ledger.json, not written here, because a hand-written count in a closing essay is exactly the claim that rots first.

Rung 2 The construction

The gap that level lowering closes

Assumption (M) gives a modular form, but of level $\mathrm{rad}(abc)$ — the product of the primes dividing $abc$. That number is enormous and entirely unknown; it depends on the solution we are trying to rule out. No contradiction can come from it directly.

Essay 24 says something quite different. The representation $\bar\rho$, as opposed to the curve, is ramified only at $2$: its conductor is $2$, full stop, regardless of how large $abc$ is. So we have one object described two ways — as coming from a form of vast level, and as having conductor $2$. Bridging that gap is precisely the content of the following theorem.

Ribet's theorem

Level lowering (Ribet, 1990). Let $p \gt 3$ be prime and let

$$ \bar\rho : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p) $$

be a continuous, odd, irreducible representation. Suppose $\bar\rho$ is finite at $p$, and that $\bar\rho$ is modular of weight $2$ and level $N$ — meaning there is a weight-2 newform of level $N$ whose associated representation is isomorphic to $\bar\rho$. Let $N(\bar\rho)$ denote the conductor of $\bar\rho$. Then $\bar\rho$ is modular of weight $2$ and level $N(\bar\rho)$.

Stated in one sentence: a modular mod $p$ representation is modular at the level its own conductor predicts. The level of the form can be pushed down to the conductor of the representation, discarding exactly the ramification that belonged to the curve rather than to the representation.

This theorem is stated here, not proved. It was conjectured by Serre in 1987 as part of a much broader programme relating mod $p$ representations to modular forms, and proved by Ribet — before Wiles, and this ordering is the historically important point. Level lowering is what made the modularity of the Frey curve worth wanting.

Applying it

Check the hypotheses against what we have. The exponent satisfies $p\gt3$. Essay 10 gives continuity and oddness, and essay 24 irreducibility for every $p\geq5$. Essay 24 assumes finiteness at $p$ and computes the conductor to be $2$. Assumption (M) makes $\bar\rho$ modular of weight $2$ and level $\operatorname{rad}(abc)$. Every hypothesis is met, so the conclusion follows:

There exists a newform of weight $2$ and level $2$.

Such a form lies in $S_2(\Gamma_0(2))$, the space of weight-2 cusp forms of level $2$. It is a non-zero element of that space — a newform is in particular a non-zero cusp form. So that space is not the zero space.

Computing the space

Now evaluate essay 13's dimension formula at $N = 2$. Four numbers are needed, and all four are elementary.

The index. $[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) : \Gamma_0(N)] = N \prod_{q \mid N}(1 + 1/q)$, so

$$ \mu \;=\; 2\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) \;=\; 3. $$

The cusps. The number of cusps of $\Gamma_0(N)$ is $\sum_{d \mid N} \varphi\bigl(\gcd(d, N/d)\bigr)$. For $N = 2$ the divisors are $1$ and $2$, each contributing $\varphi(1) = 1$, so

$$ \nu_\infty \;=\; 2. $$

Concretely these are the cusps $\infty$ and $0$.

The elliptic points. For $N = 2$ there is one elliptic point of order two and none of order three:

$$ \nu_2 \;=\; 1, \qquad \nu_3 \;=\; 0. $$

Substituting,

$$ \dim S_2(\Gamma_0(2)) \;=\; 1 + \frac{3}{12} - \frac{1}{4} - \frac{0}{3} - \frac{2}{2} \;=\; 1 + \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{4} - 1 \;=\; 0. $$

The space is zero-dimensional. There is no weight-2 cusp form of level $2$ at all, let alone a newform. $\blacksquare$

Rung 3 What it buys

The chain collapses

Read the contradiction backwards, one link at a time.

  • The space $S_2(\Gamma_0(2))$ is zero, so no newform of weight 2 and level 2 exists.
  • Ribet's theorem says that if $\bar\rho$ existed with the stated properties, such a newform would exist. So $\bar\rho$ does not exist.
  • But $\bar\rho$ was constructed from the mod $p$ torsion of $E$, and would exist if $E$ did. So $E$ does not exist.
  • And $E$ was constructed from the solution by an explicit formula, so it would exist if the solution did. So the solution does not exist.

The modular contradiction applies to every prime $p\geq5$ — no exponent in that range is excluded, which was not true of earlier drafts of this collection. Essay 01 reduced every $n\geq3$ to $n=4$ or an odd prime and settled $n=4$; essay 02 settles the first case of $n=3$ and outlines the second. So once that outline becomes a proof, no exponent $n\geq3$ survives: Fermat's Last Theorem. Today that last sentence is conditional on (M), Ribet and essay 02, exactly as the ledger records — and on nothing else. $\blacksquare$

How much room there was

A natural worry about an argument that ends in a zero is whether it only barely ends there. It does not. The script checks/dim_s2_gamma0.py evaluates the same formula for every level and reports that $\dim S_2(\Gamma_0(N)) = 0$ for

$$ N = 1, 2, \dots, 10,\; 12,\; 13,\; 16,\; 18,\; 25, $$

cross-checked against the classical list of levels at which it vanishes. Level $2$ sits well inside that range, not at its edge. Had the Frey representation's conductor come out as $3$ or $4$ or $5$ instead, the contradiction would have arrived just the same. What matters is not the exact value $2$ but that level lowering drives the level down to a small number, into a range where the spaces are empty for the boring reason that there is no room in them.

The same script also confirms $\dim S_2(\Gamma_0(11)) = 1$, matching the LMFDB entry for the newform labelled 11.2.a.a — so the formula that returns zero at level $2$ is the same one that correctly returns one at level $11$, where a form demonstrably does exist. A dimension formula that only ever produced zeroes would prove nothing.

What the proof does not give

It is worth being precise about the shape of what has been achieved, because a proof by contradiction of a non-existence statement is a peculiar kind of knowledge.

No bounds, no near-misses, no algorithm. The argument shows a solution cannot exist. It offers no way to measure how badly the equation fails, no bound on how close $a^p + b^p$ can come to a $p$-th power. Contrast essay 02's numerical verification below four million: that was weaker logically but richer computationally.

No explanation. There is no sense in which this proof says why Fermat's equation has no solutions. It says that a solution would force two rigid structures — one geometric, one analytic — to agree in a place where one of them is empty. That is a reason, but it is not the kind of reason the question seems to ask for, and it is certainly not what Fermat had in mind in his margin.

The general question is still open. Fermat's equation is one case of a family of Diophantine questions about which far less is known. The ABC conjecture, which would imply results of Fermat's kind for a whole class of equations at once, remains unsettled.

And modularity has since grown. Wiles's theorem covered semistable curves, which is all the Frey curve needs. Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor removed the hypothesis entirely in 2001: every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is modular. The tool built for one 350-year-old problem turned out to be a general theorem about the arithmetic of curves, and that — rather than Fermat's equation — is what the proof is now used for.

Rung 4 Reading

Level lowering. Ribet's 1990 paper in Inventiones Mathematicae, on modular representations arising from modular forms, is the source; Serre's 1987 paper is where the conjecture was formulated. Darmon, Diamond and Taylor's survey Fermat's Last Theorem recounts the reduction in its introduction and treats Serre's conjectures in its third chapter, with the ramification of the Frey representation stated precisely as its theorem 2.15.

The dimension formula. Diamond and Shurman's A First Course in Modular Forms develops the index, cusp and elliptic-point counts for $\Gamma_0(N)$ and the genus formula they feed.

The form that does exist. LMFDB newform 11.2.a.a: level $11$, weight $2$, trivial character, dimension $1$, with coefficients beginning $1, -2, -1, 2, 1, 2, -2$. It is attached to the elliptic curve isogeny class 11.a — the smallest conductor for which a curve exists at all, and the standard first example of modularity in action.

Check it yourself. Run checks/dim_s2_gamma0.py. The four numbers $\mu = 3$, $\nu_2 = 1$, $\nu_3 = 0$, $\nu_\infty = 2$ are each computable in a line, and the formula then gives $0$ in one step. It is a genuinely small calculation, and the whole 350-year-old problem rests on it — which is the most surprising thing about the proof and the reason this collection is named after it.
